## Executive Summary & Nutrient Recommendations
The ICMR-NIN guidelines establish evidence-based dietary recommendations tailored to Indian physiology, genetic predispositions, and traditional dietary patterns. A central recommendation is addressing the prevailing cereal-heavy, protein-deficient Indian dietary landscape.

### 1. Daily Protein Requirements & Distribution
- **Sedentary Adults**: 0.83 g per kilogram of body weight per day of high-quality protein (or corrected for quality). For an average 65 kg adult, minimum daily protein intake should be 54–65 g, increasing to 1.2–2.0 g/kg for resistance training or fat-loss phases.
- **Macronutrient Energy Distribution**:
  - Carbohydrates: 50% to 60% of total daily energy (limiting simple sugars and refined grains to <5%).
  - Protein: 15% to 20% of total energy.
  - Fat: 20% to 30% of total energy, emphasizing polyunsaturated and monounsaturated fatty acids with a balanced n-6 to n-3 fatty acid ratio (5:1 to 10:1).
  - Dietary Fiber: Minimum 30 g to 40 g per 2,000 kcal diet from unrefined grains, legumes, vegetables, and seeds.

### 2. Cereal-Pulse Complementary Protein Ratios
Traditional Indian plant-based diets rely on cereals (rice, wheat, millets) and pulses (dals, rajma, chana). Cereals are inherently limiting in the essential amino acid lysine but rich in methionine. Conversely, pulses and legumes are abundant in lysine but limiting in methionine and cysteine.
- **Optimal Ratio**: Consuming cereals and pulses in a **3:1 to 4:1 ratio** by weight provides a complete essential amino acid profile with a Biological Value (BV) approaching animal proteins (PDCAAS ~0.75–0.85).
- Common optimal combinations include:
  - *Khichdi* (Rice + Moong Dal)
  - *Roti + Dal Tadka* (Whole wheat + Toor/Chana dal)
  - *Idli / Dosa* (Fermented Parboiled Rice + Urad Dal in 3:1 ratio)

### 3. Micronutrient Priority Gaps in Indian Diets
- **Iron**: Anemia affects over 50% of Indian women and a substantial proportion of men due to low bioavailability of non-heme plant iron.
- **Calcium**: Dairy products (curd, paneer, milk), ragi (finger millet), and sesame seeds (til) provide essential calcium (target: 1,000 mg/day).
- **Vitamin B12**: Almost exclusively synthesized by microorganisms and absent in unfortified plant foods. Strict vegetarians have a >70% prevalence of subclinical or overt B12 deficiency.
- **Vitamin D3**: Despite ample sunshine, urban lifestyle and melanin pigmentation lead to widespread deficiency (<20 ng/mL).

### 4. Practical Implementation for Body Recomposition
When undergoing caloric restriction ("cutting"):
- Increase relative protein intake to 1.6–2.2 g/kg to preserve fat-free mass (FFM).
- Prioritize high protein-density foods: Low-fat paneer, Greek curd/yogurt, roasted chana, soya chunks (52% protein by dry weight), egg whites, and whey protein isolate.
