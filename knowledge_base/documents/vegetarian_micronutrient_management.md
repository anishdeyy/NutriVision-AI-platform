## Critical Micronutrient Risk Profiles

### 1. Non-Heme Iron & Enhancers/Inhibitors
Plant-based foods contain non-heme iron ($Fe^{3+}$), which has an average absorption rate of only 2% to 10%, compared to 15% to 35% for animal heme iron ($Fe^{2+}$).
- **Potent Enhancers**:
  - **Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C)**: Co-ingestion of 50–100 mg Vitamin C (e.g., fresh lemon juice squeezed on dal, amla, raw bell peppers, tomato) reduces ferric iron to ferrous iron and prevents insoluble chelation, increasing iron absorption by 200% to 400%.
- **Potent Inhibitors**:
  - **Tannins and Polyphenols**: Consuming tea (*chai*) or coffee within 1 hour of main meals reduces non-heme iron absorption by 60% to 70%.
  - **Phytic Acid**: Present in unfermented whole wheat bran and unsoaked pulses.

### 2. Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)
- **Physiology**: Vitamin B12 is synthesized by gut microbes in animals and does not naturally occur in plant foods unless fortified.
- **RDA**: 2.2 to 2.5 mcg/day for Indian adults.
- **Dietary Sources**: Whole milk, curd, paneer (provide ~0.3–0.5 mcg per 100g serving; requires substantial dairy intake to achieve RDA), eggs (for eggetarians, 1 egg provides ~0.6 mcg).
- **Clinical Implication**: Strict vegetarians and vegans with low dairy consumption should be advised that dietary gaps in B12 are highly probable and testing/supplementation should be evaluated with a qualified healthcare professional.

### 3. Calcium & Vitamin D Synergy
- **Calcium RDA**: 1,000 mg/day for men and women.
- **Indian Super-sources**:
  - Ragi (Finger Millet): 344 mg calcium per 100 g.
  - White Sesame Seeds (*Til*): 975 mg calcium per 100 g.
  - Low-fat Paneer: ~480 mg per 100 g.
  - Curd: ~150 mg per 100 g.
- **Vitamin D**: Essential for active intestinal calcium transport. Adequate sun exposure (midday 15–20 mins) or dietary supplementation is critical because food sources (mushrooms, fortified milk) are limited in unfortified Indian staples.

### 4. Omega-3 Fatty Acids (ALA vs. EPA/DHA)
Vegetarians rely on alpha-linolenic acid (ALA) from flaxseeds (*alsi*), chia seeds, and walnuts. Endogenous hepatic conversion of ALA to EPA and DHA is relatively low (~5% to 8% for EPA, <1% for DHA). Incorporating 15 g crushed flaxseed or chia seeds daily helps satisfy essential n-3 fatty acid targets.
