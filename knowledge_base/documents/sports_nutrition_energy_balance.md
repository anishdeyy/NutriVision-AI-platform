## Core Nutritional Mechanics

### 1. Energy Deficit & Surplus Quantification
- **Cutting (Fat Loss)**: An energy deficit of 300–500 kcal/day below Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) targets ~0.5% to 1.0% of total body weight loss per week, minimizing skeletal muscle catabolism.
- **Bulking (Hypertrophy)**: A moderate caloric surplus of 250–350 kcal/day promotes lean mass accrual with minimal adipose storage.
- **TDEE Formula (Mifflin-St Jeor)**:
  - $\text{BMR}_{\text{male}} = 10 \times \text{weight (kg)} + 6.25 \times \text{height (cm)} - 5 \times \text{age (yrs)} + 5$
  - $\text{BMR}_{\text{female}} = 10 \times \text{weight (kg)} + 6.25 \times \text{height (cm)} - 5 \times \text{age (yrs)} - 161$
  - $\text{TDEE} = \text{BMR} \times \text{Activity Factor}$ (1.2: Sedentary, 1.375: Light, 1.55: Moderate, 1.725: Very Active).

### 2. Leucine Threshold and Meal Distribution
- Muscle Protein Synthesis (MPS) is triggered by an intracellular surge of the essential amino acid **leucine** (the "leucine trigger").
- An effective meal dose requires approximately **2.5 to 3.0 g of leucine**, equivalent to:
  - 25–30 g Whey Protein
  - 120 g Chicken Breast
  - 4 Whole Eggs
  - 150 g Paneer
  - 40 g Soya Chunks
- Pacing protein intake every **3.5 to 5 hours** across 3 to 5 meals achieves repeated maximal stimulation of fractional synthetic rate (FSR) compared to skewing all protein into a single large meal.

### 3. Satiety and Caloric Density During Cutting
When operating in a negative energy balance:
- Protein has the highest thermic effect of food (TEF: 20–30% of energy consumed), boosting metabolic throughput.
- Dietary fiber (>30 g/day) delays gastric emptying and stimulates satiety hormones GLP-1 and PYY.
- Consuming high-volume, low-calorie items (cucumber salads, vegetable curries with minimal added ghee/oil, roasted makhana, clear vegetable soups) prevents adherence fatigue.
