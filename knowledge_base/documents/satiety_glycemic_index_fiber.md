## Key Findings & Principles

### 1. Glycemic Index (GI) vs. Glycemic Load (GL)
Traditional Indian refined cereals (polished white rice, maida naan) exhibit a high Glycemic Index (GI >70), triggering rapid postprandial glucose spikes followed by reactive hypoglycemia and premature hunger rebound within 90–120 minutes.
- **Lowering Meal Glycemic Load**:
  - Combining polished rice with fiber-rich dals and cooked green leafy vegetables drops total meal GI to <55.
  - Adding fat and protein (e.g., paneer, curd, or eggs) slows gastric emptying rate, blunting glucose excursions.
  - Parboiled rice (*usna chawal*) or whole wheat with retained bran preserves resistant starch.

### 2. Viscous and Fermentable Fiber
- **Soluble Fiber Sources**: Oats, barley, fenugreek seeds (*methi dana*), psyllium husk (*isabgol*), and chia seeds form a viscous gel in the upper gastrointestinal tract, attenuating glucose and lipid absorption.
- **Microbiome Fermentation**: Colonic fermentation of resistant starch and oligosaccharides from legumes produces Short-Chain Fatty Acids (SCFAs: acetate, propionate, butyrate), which directly stimulate enteroendocrine L-cells to release peptide YY (PYY) and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1), downregulating hypothalamic appetite signaling.

### 3. Practical Recommendations for Indian Meal Composition
- Fill 50% of the plate with non-starchy vegetables (cucumber, tomato, spinach, cabbage, bhindi, bottle gourd).
- Allocate 25% of the plate to protein-dense foods (paneer, tofu, soya chunks, dal/legumes, chicken, fish, eggs).
- Allocate 25% of the plate to complex whole carbohydrates (whole wheat roti, brown rice, millets: bajra/jowar/ragi).
