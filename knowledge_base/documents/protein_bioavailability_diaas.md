## Core Scientific Principles

### 1. Understanding Effective Protein vs. Crude Protein
Nutritional labeling displays **crude protein** calculated by Kjeldahl nitrogen determination ($N \times 6.25$). However, metabolic utilization is constrained by:
1. True Ileal Amino Acid Digestibility (DIAAS score).
2. Limiting essential amino acids relative to human tissue synthesis requirements.
3. Presence of plant antinutritional factors (phytates, polyphenols, trypsin inhibitors, lectins).

### 2. Relative Bioavailability & Quality Multipliers
For practical nutrition intelligence, the application models effective bioavailable protein using empirical consensus multipliers:
- **Whole Eggs, Chicken Breast, Fish**: Quality Index = **1.0 (100%)**. DIAAS scores range from 1.15 to 1.30. Digestibility >95%.
- **Dairy (Paneer, Curd, Greek Yogurt, Milk)**: Quality Index = **0.80–0.90 (80–90%)**. Casein and whey provide high branched-chain amino acids (BCAA) and high net protein utilization.
- **Soya Chunks, Tofu, Isolated Soy Protein**: Quality Index = **0.70–0.75 (70–75%)**. Complete plant protein, though heat treatment in industrial processing is required to deactivate trypsin inhibitors.
- **Pulses & Legumes (Dal Tadka, Rajma, Chana, Moong)**: Quality Index = **0.60–0.65 (60–65%)**. Moderate digestibility due to intact cell walls and phytate-bound minerals.
- **Cereals & Millets (Wheat Roti, White/Brown Rice, Bajra, Jowar)**: Quality Index = **0.40–0.50 (40–50%)**. Severely limited by lysine; true ileal utilization is lower without complementary pulse co-ingestion.

### 3. Mitigation of Antinutritional Factors
Traditional Indian food preparation significantly alters bioaccessibility:
- **Soaking legumes for 12–16 hours**: Reduces soluble phytate content by 25–40%.
- **Germination / Sprouting (Sprouts Salad, sprouted moong)**: Upregulates endogenous phytase enzymes, increasing iron bioavailability by up to 300% and protein digestibility by 18–25%.
- **Pressure Cooking & Fermentation (Idli/Dosa batter)**: Deactivates heat-labile trypsin inhibitors and degrades antinutrients through microbial lactic fermentation.

### 4. Mathematical Estimation Formulation
$$ \text{Effective Protein} (g) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \text{Crude Protein}_i \times \text{Bioavailability Score}_i \right) $$
*Nutritional Note*: Effective protein provides users an evidence-grounded estimate of actual tissue-available protein, countering the common misconception that 100 g raw dal yields equivalent muscle synthesis support to 100 g dairy or egg protein.
